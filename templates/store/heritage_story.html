The Cire-Perdue Foundry Chronicle

How molten virgin brass, pure organic beeswax, and Cauvery riverbed silt combine to form sacred, eternal heirlooms that withstand centuries.

Chapter I • The Shilpa Shastras

Sacred Iconometry & Proportions

In ancient Vedic casting, a sculpture is not merely decorative; it is a resonant geometric vessel. Our master Sthapatis in Swamimalai and Thanjavur follow the Talamana system, an intricate proportional canon where every deity's gaze, stance (tribhanga), and hand mudra is calibrated down to the millimeter.

From the cosmic arch of the Nataraja to the benevolent pot-belly of Lord Vinayaka, every curve honors celestial harmony.

Chapter II • The Crucible Sacrifice

Lost-Wax: Sacrificed for Permanence

The name Cire Perdue reflects the ultimate sacrifice: the beeswax original is completely melted away and consumed by red-hot liquefied brass at 980°C. Because the mold is destroyed upon cooling to retrieve the bronze core, each finished piece is truly unique in the world.

The Metallurgical Standard

85% Virgin Copper ingots combined with 15% Zinc produce the resonant density and golden warm luster distinctive to AmberCast atelier artworks.

Chapter III • Chasing & Natural Patination

Over 70 Hours of Manual Chisel Work

Once cast, the raw metal plinth undergoes four days of meticulous hand filing, engraving, and chasing. Small steel chisels carve out eye pupils, fingernails, flowing silk garments, and flame crowns. Finally, the brass is oxidized using natural tamarind pulp, coconut husk charcoal, and mustard oil to achieve a warm antique ochre sheen.
